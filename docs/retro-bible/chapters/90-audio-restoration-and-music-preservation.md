# Audio Restoration and Music Preservation

Game audio preservation is more than keeping a soundtrack folder.

This chapter is the skeleton for music rips, streamed audio, MIDI, chip sound, surround mixes, voice acting and the fragile context around how games sounded.

## Historical Context

Game audio moved from chips and simple samples through CD audio, MIDI modules, streamed files and full orchestral recordings. Each era has different preservation needs.

## Practical Advice

- Preserve original game audio paths.
- Keep soundtrack releases separate from in-game audio.
- Document MIDI device choices.
- Avoid over-cleaning noise that belongs to the hardware.
- Record audio fixes and patches per game.

## Preservation Notes

For some games, the difference between AdLib, Sound Blaster and MT-32 is not cosmetic. It is part of the experience.

> **DAP Tip**
>
> When preserving PC games, write down the intended audio device. Future-you will not remember which MIDI option sounded right.

## Common Mistakes

- Treating soundtrack albums as replacements for game audio.
- Losing CD audio tracks during conversion.
- Ignoring loop points.
- Applying noise reduction too aggressively.
- Forgetting speech packs and language variants.

## Troubleshooting

### Music is missing

Check disc audio tracks, cue files, mounted CD paths and emulator audio settings.

### MIDI sounds wrong

Check selected device, soundfont, MT-32 mode and game setup utility.
