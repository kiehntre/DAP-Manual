# Sega Master System

The Master System is a reminder that console history looks different depending on where you lived.

This chapter is the skeleton for Sega's 8-bit console, with special attention to regional libraries and the places where it mattered most.

## History

The Master System competed with the NES, but its story was not the same worldwide. In Brazil and parts of Europe, it had a much longer and stronger life than many North American histories suggest.

## Hardware Overview

- 8-bit cartridge console.
- Card media on some early models and regions.
- Related to SG-1000 and Game Gear in important ways.
- Regional BIOS behaviour can matter.

## Why It Mattered

It carried Sega's arcade energy into the home and built a foundation for the Mega Drive era.

## Preservation Status

Generally strong. Regional variants, BIOS behaviour and lesser-known releases need care.

## BIOS Requirements

Some emulator modes and regional behaviours may use BIOS files. Standard cartridge play often works without them.

## Recommended File Formats

- `.sms` for Master System ROM images.
- Keep SG-1000 and Game Gear separate.

## Folder Layout

```text
/mnt/games/roms/mastersystem/
/mnt/games/bios/mastersystem/
/mnt/games/saves/mastersystem/
```

## Enhancements

CRT shaders and clean integer scaling work well. Avoid making the system look like a Mega Drive by accident.

## Common Problems

- Region confusion.
- BIOS boot behaviour unexpected.
- SG-1000 titles mixed into Master System folders.
- Controller button mapping too modernised.

> **DAP Tip**
>
> When writing Master System notes, include region context. The system's legacy is not one-size-fits-all.
