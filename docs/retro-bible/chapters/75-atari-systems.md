# Atari Systems

Atari is not one system. It is a whole shelf of different eras.

This chapter is the skeleton for Atari coverage across the 2600, 5200, 7800, Lynx, Jaguar, ST and related machines, with later passes deciding which deserve deeper standalone chapters.

## History

Atari helped define the arcade, the early home console market and parts of home computing. Its name also carries the weight of industry crashes, reinventions and missed chances.

## Hardware Overview

| Family | Notes |
| --- | --- |
| Atari 2600 | Early cartridge console with huge historical importance. |
| Atari 5200/7800 | Follow-up consoles with smaller but relevant libraries. |
| Atari Lynx | Colour handheld with impressive hardware. |
| Atari Jaguar | Ambitious and difficult 1990s console. |
| Atari ST | Home computer line important for games and music. |

## Why It Mattered

Atari is foundational. Even when later systems struggled, the earlier impact shaped the language of games.

## Preservation Status

Varies heavily by platform. Cartridge systems are generally easier; computers and Jaguar material need more care.

## Recommended Emulator

Stella for Atari 2600, Hatari for Atari ST, and system-specific emulators for Lynx and Jaguar.

## BIOS Requirements

Some Atari systems need BIOS or OS ROMs. Document per platform.

## Recommended File Formats

- `.a26`, `.bin` and related cartridge formats.
- Disk images for ST.
- Keep platforms separate.

## Folder Layout

```text
/mnt/games/roms/atari2600/
/mnt/games/roms/atarist/
/mnt/games/roms/atarilynx/
/mnt/games/roms/atarijaguar/
```

## Common Problems

- Platforms collapsed into one Atari folder.
- Controller type not set.
- Computer disk images treated like console cartridges.

> **DAP Tip**
>
> Give Atari systems separate folders. The brand is shared; the machines are not.

## DAP Gold Standard Setup

- Platform-specific emulator choices.
- BIOS and OS ROMs verified where needed.
- Controller notes per system.
- Computer media kept separate from console media.
