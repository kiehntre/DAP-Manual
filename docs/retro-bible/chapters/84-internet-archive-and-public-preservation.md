# Internet Archive and Public Preservation

Public preservation is messy, necessary and often misunderstood.

This chapter is the skeleton for discussing the Internet Archive and similar public efforts without pretending they replace personal ownership, verification or careful legal thinking.

## Historical Context

The Internet Archive became one of the web's most important memory institutions. For games, software, manuals, websites and store pages, it often preserves context that would otherwise disappear.

## Practical Advice

- Use public archives for research and context.
- Prefer official or primary sources when available.
- Save metadata, manuals and pages that explain a release.
- Do not treat every archived file as verified preservation.
- Keep legal boundaries clear.

## Preservation Notes

Public archives are especially valuable for:

- manuals;
- catalogues;
- patch notes;
- store pages;
- interviews;
- magazines;
- old websites;
- software history.

> **DAP Tip**
>
> An archived page can be as important as a dumped game. Context is part of preservation.

## Common Mistakes

- Treating archive presence as legal permission.
- Assuming uploaded files are verified.
- Forgetting to cite archived context.
- Ignoring takedown and access changes.

## Troubleshooting

### Source link is dead

Check archived versions, official mirrors, repository tags and documentation snapshots.

### Archived file identity is unclear

Check checksum, provenance, upload notes and whether a preservation project has a matching record.

## DAP Warning

Do not build the book on shaky links. Archive important sources, but still cite responsibly.
