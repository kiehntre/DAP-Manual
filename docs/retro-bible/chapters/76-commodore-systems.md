# Commodore Systems

Commodore preservation is not a single emulator checkbox. It is tapes, disks, keyboards, joysticks, monitors and a culture of home computing.

This chapter is the skeleton for Commodore systems, especially the C64, while pointing the Amiga to its own chapter.

## History

Commodore machines brought computing and games into homes across the world. The C64 in particular became a long-lived platform with commercial games, demos, magazines and bedroom programming.

## Hardware Overview

- Keyboard-based home computers.
- Tape, disk and cartridge media.
- Joystick ports.
- Regional video standards.
- SID sound on the C64.

## Why It Mattered

Commodore machines were not just game consoles. They were places where players became tinkerers.

## Preservation Status

Broad but messy. TOSEC-style cataloguing helps because the ecosystem includes cracks, trainers, demos, cover disks and applications.

## Recommended Emulator

VICE is the core recommendation for C64 and related 8-bit Commodore systems.

## BIOS Requirements

Kernel, BASIC and drive ROMs may be needed depending on emulator packaging and target machine.

## Recommended File Formats

- `.d64`, `.g64`, `.tap`, `.crt` and related formats.
- Keep originals and working copies separate.

## Folder Layout

```text
/mnt/games/roms/c64/
/mnt/games/bios/commodore/
/mnt/games/computers/c64-notes/
```

## Enhancements

Display and SID settings matter. Avoid smoothing the character out of the machine.

## Common Problems

- Not knowing load commands.
- Disk side swapping.
- Joystick port wrong.
- PAL and NTSC mismatch.

> **DAP Tip**
>
> Computer systems need notes. A game that requires a typed command or joystick port change should say so before launch.

## DAP Gold Standard Setup

- VICE configured.
- ROMs and drive settings documented.
- Media type retained.
- Loading notes stored with difficult titles.
