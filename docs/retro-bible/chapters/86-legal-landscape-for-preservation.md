# Legal Landscape for Preservation

The legal side of preservation is where people either get too casual or too frightened to say anything useful.

This chapter is the skeleton for careful, non-lawyer guidance that separates ownership, dumping, emulation, distribution, BIOS files, keys and documentation.

## Historical Context

Games moved from cartridges and discs into accounts, servers, patches and DLC. The law did not become simpler as the technology became more layered.

## Practical Advice

- Do not distribute copyrighted games, BIOS files, firmware or keys.
- Keep personal ownership notes private.
- Separate technical explanation from legal permission.
- Cite official policies where possible.
- Avoid universal claims across countries.

## Key Distinctions

| Topic | Draft position |
| --- | --- |
| Emulators | Usually legal as software, but facts matter. |
| ROMs and disc images | Distribution is the major risk. |
| BIOS and firmware | Copyrighted system software. |
| Keys and licences | Treat with strict care. |
| Metadata and manuals | Rights still apply. |

> **DAP Warning**
>
> This book is not legal advice. It should explain risk clearly and avoid giving readers false confidence.

## Preservation Notes

Legal caution should not erase history. The book can discuss formats, workflows and risks without providing infringing material.

## Common Mistakes

- Calling every download preservation.
- Assuming old means public domain.
- Sharing BIOS files casually.
- Publishing private account or licence details.
- Treating one country's exception as universal.

## Troubleshooting

### A workflow feels legally unclear

Stop, document the technical need, check primary sources and avoid publishing links or instructions that cross the line.
